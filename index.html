Fels Capstone · City of Philadelphia, Division of Development Services · 2026

The Mixed Income Neighborhoods Overlay and Residential Development in Philadelphia

Philadelphia's MIN overlay requires residential projects of 10 or more units to set aside 20% of units as affordable at 40% of area median income. Developers warned it would stall construction. Raw trends seemed to agree: permits fell inside the overlay, and the share of projects just under the 10-unit threshold roughly doubled.

After comparing MIN census tracts to matched tracts with similar income, tenure, vacancy, and pre-policy permitting, the effects on permit volume, project size, and threshold avoidance were statistically indistinguishable from zero. The slowdown fits citywide forces better, including the 2021 tax-abatement redesign and higher financing costs after 2022. The report recommends keeping the overlay as designed and re-evaluating it as more data accumulates.

Fox Leadership Research Fellowship · Office of Children and Families · 2025–26

Did Philadelphia's 2017 reforms narrow racial disparities in foster care?

I built a 15-year county panel from federal AFCARS case records: more than 9 million observations across 220+ variables, merged with Census ACS population estimates. I used it to compare Philadelphia with counties that resembled it before the city's 2017 intake reforms.

Relative to 20 matched counties, Philadelphia's Black-to-White placement disparity ratio fell by about 1.0 point after the reforms (p = 0.013). Separately, the city's point-in-time caseload fell from 2.4 times the comparator average in FY2015 to 1.4 times in FY2023. That decline began before 2017, so the trend chart describes the change but does not establish a cause. I presented the results to OCF leadership in an office-wide briefing.

Senior Independent Study · The College of Wooster · 2021

The Great Outdoors, Indoors: Green Spaces and Housing Prices in Pittsburgh, PA

I developed a consumer model of green-space preference and estimated the specific effect using a spatial autoregressive moving average (SARMA) hedonic model on single-family home sales in Allegheny County. Sale prices fall by 2.34% for each additional mile from a park, playground, or athletic field. That premium on park access widens the gap between high- and low-income neighborhoods.
